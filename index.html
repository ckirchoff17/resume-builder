Résumé Brainstorming Workbook

You've probably done more than you think. List everything first, then turn it into a résumé draft and plan what you'd like to add next.

How to use this workbook

Each part builds on the one before it. Everything you list in Part 1 flows into your résumé draft in Part 2, and Part 3 helps you see what you'd like to add.

  1. Brainstorm. Go category by category and write down everything you've done, even things that don't feel important.
  2. Draft my résumé. Choose what to include and put it in order. Your résumé builds itself as you go, ready to print or copy.
  3. Plan what's next. Look for gaps, set a goal or two, and find the guide that matches your interests.

Where to focus in your grade

9th grade

Your list will be short, and that's expected. Spend your time in Part 1 and Part 3, and think of this year as exploring.

10th grade

Add what you tried last year and notice what you kept doing. Use Part 3 to go deeper in one or two areas you care about.

11th grade

Build your full draft in Part 2. Focus on what you did and the difference it made, with numbers wherever you can.

12th grade

Polish your draft for applications and interviews. Use the Common App fields in Part 1 to fill in your activities list.

Your answers stay on this device. The workbook saves automatically in this browser, and nothing is sent anywhere. If you switch computers or clear your browser, your work won't come with you, so use Download backup now and then and Restore backup to pick up where you left off.

Part 1: Brainstorm

Write down everything, then decide later what goes on your résumé. If you're not sure something counts, add it anyway. Babysitting your cousins every weekend counts. So does the YouTube channel nobody watches yet.

Help with describing what you did
Leading
Led, organized, coordinated, directed, founded, mentored
Creating
Designed, built, wrote, produced, launched, developed
Helping
Supported, tutored, served, assisted, cared for, volunteered
Improving
Increased, raised, grew, streamlined, expanded, improved
Communicating
Presented, taught, translated, promoted, recruited, edited
Figuring things out
Researched, analyzed, solved, tested, planned, managed

Start each line with a verb, then add a number if you can. "Helped with the bake sale" becomes "Organized a bake sale with 8 volunteers that raised $650 for the food pantry."

Skills

Languages you speak or are learning, software and tools you know, and certifications like CPR, lifeguarding, or food handler.
